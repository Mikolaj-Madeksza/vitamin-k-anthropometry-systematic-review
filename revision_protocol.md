# Major revision protocol

## Vitamin K supplementation and anthropometric outcomes: systematic review of randomized controlled trials

**Date:** 6 November 2026  
**Status:** Protocol for a substantial *post-peer-review methodological revision*. This document was prepared after the original search, extraction, analysis, manuscript submission, and receipt of reviewer comments.
**Original registration:** PROSPERO CRD420261278785
**Review team:** M.S. and M.M. (review, extraction, and assessment); W.M. (adjudication); ChatGPT-6 (OpenAI; independent AI-assisted verification, with the exact deployed model identifier and configuration recorded).  

## 1. Purpose and principles
The review examines the effect of vitamin K supplementation on anthropometric and body-composition outcomes in randomized controlled trials (RCTs).

The guiding principles are:
1. **Distinguish original work from new work.** Preserve the original PROSPERO record, original exports, decisions, extractions, and manuscript. Never retrospectively describe revised methods as having been used in the original review.
2. **Treat trials, not publications, as the independent units.** Link all reports of the same randomized population; use each report to obtain complementary information without counting participants or effects twice.
3. **Prioritize randomized between-group treatment effects.** Do not infer efficacy from within-group change or from the number of studies with statistically significant P values.
4. **Make human responsibility explicit.** AI provides a separate verification judgment, not an authoritative final decision. Human reviewers and W.M. retain responsibility for the research record and its interpretation.
5. **Record every decision needed to reproduce the synthesis.** Keep source locations, extraction corrections, calculation rules, risk-of-bias reasoning, and code under version control.
6. **Keep the published article concise.** Place methods needed for auditability, detailed data, and supplementary analyses in the supplementary information or public repository, while retaining the essentials in the main text.

## 2. Eligibility and review question

### 2.1 Population
- Human adults aged 18 years or older, of any sex, race/ethnicity, BMI, baseline vitamin K status, or medical condition. The shift from the original age ≥10 years threshold to ≥18 years is a **post-review amendment**.
- Exclude pregnant participants because pregnancy-related physiological changes make anthropometric effects difficult to interpret for this review's question. Mixed adult/pediatric or pregnant/nonpregnant samples are eligible only when separate eligible-subgroup results can be obtained, or the ineligible participants do not materially affect the relevant result under an explicitly documented rule.
- Do not exclude populations for having diabetes, PCOS, osteopenia, obesity, or another chronic condition. Capture population characteristics for interpretation.

### 2.2 Intervention and comparator
- Supplementation with any form of vitamin K, including K1, K2 menaquinones (e.g., MK-4 and MK-7), and other forms, at any dose or route if intended as supplementation.
- Intervention duration at least four weeks.
- Comparator: placebo, no vitamin K supplementation, or usual care, provided there is a randomized difference attributable to vitamin K.
- Co-interventions (including vitamin D, calcium, multivitamins, diet advice, or other agents) are permitted only when balanced between the relevant randomized groups. Record the complete composition of both arms; do not describe a common co-intervention as if it were control-only.
- For multiarm trials, identify every eligible contrast, avoid double counting a shared comparator, and document the primary treatment contrast and any dose-specific analyses.

### 2.3 Study design and reports
- Include randomized parallel-group trials and other randomized designs where an interpretable eligible vitamin K contrast is available. Record design features and use the appropriate methods for nonstandard designs.
- Exclude nonrandomized studies, observational studies, single-arm studies, animal/in-vitro research, reviews, and studies with intervention periods shorter than four weeks.
- Include all reports, companion papers, registry records, supplementary appendices, dissertations or other sources that provide eligible trial data. They are reports of a trial, not additional independent trials.
- No language or publication-status restriction at search or screening. Translate potentially eligible non-English material where feasible; log unavailable translations and reports.
- Eligible trials must have measured at least one relevant post-intervention anthropometric/body-composition outcome. Trials with confirmed measurement but unavailable results are included in the review and study-characteristics table, labelled as having unavailable outcome data, and excluded from synthesis. Planned outcomes or baseline BMI alone do not establish eligible follow-up measurement.

### 2.4 Outcomes and hierarchy
| Priority | Outcome domain | Individual outcomes |
|---|---|---|
| Main | General anthropometry | Body weight (kg); BMI (kg/m²) |
| Main | Central adiposity | Waist circumference (cm) |
| Main | Total adiposity | Fat mass (kg); body-fat percentage (%) |
| Secondary | Fat distribution | Waist-to-hip ratio, hip circumference, visceral adipose tissue, regional fat, fat mass index |
| Exploratory | Lean tissue | Lean mass, skeletal muscle mass, related body-composition indices |

Height is not an outcome of interest except where needed to define BMI. Treat fat mass in kilograms and body-fat percentage as distinct outcomes; do not convert a ratio of group means into a purported mean participant-level body-fat percentage. Record measurement methods (DXA, BIA, imaging, tape measurement, etc.) and their comparability.

This expanded outcome hierarchy is a **post-review amendment**. The original five outcomes must be identified in the amendment log. Do not silently elevate outcomes after inspecting which results were favorable.

### 2.5 Outcome timing and analytic priority
- The primary time point is the end of the randomized supplementation period (longest prespecified assessment during the allocated intervention). Record other available time points separately.
- Prefer the authors' prespecified, appropriately adjusted between-group estimate of the effect of randomized assignment, ideally an intention-to-treat analysis; then other between-group estimates. Document the hierarchy and every deviation for individual studies.
- Separate overall randomized comparisons from within-group changes, post hoc subgroup analyses, and biomarker-defined post-randomization responder comparisons.
- Extract whether each anthropometric outcome was a primary, secondary, exploratory, or unclassified trial outcome, and whether sample-size calculations targeted that outcome. Do not infer power from a nonsignificant P value.

## 3. Search reconstruction and update

### 3.1 Information sources
Search four bibliographic databases from inception to the revised search date:
1. PubMed/MEDLINE;
2. Scopus;
3. Web of Science Core Collection;
4. Cochrane CENTRAL.

Supplement these searches with backward and forward citation searching of eligible reports and key reviews, and ClinicalTrials.gov/WHO ICTRP checks where accessible. Check related registrations and supplementary material for missing results. Record each source's platform, date, coverage, query, count, and access/export limitations.

Embase was removed before formal execution because searches failed in the available institutional interface, confirmed by M.M. on 6 November 2026. ProQuest remains an original-review source only. Do not describe either as searched in the revision.

### 3.2 Search design
- Search vitamin K names, forms, spelling variants, and subject headings; correct `menaquionon*` to `menaquinon*`.
- In PubMed, Scopus and Web of Science, combine the vitamin K concept with platform-specific trial-identification terms based on established filters. Document all adaptations and their local recall checks; do not call an adapted strategy independently validated. CENTRAL requires no additional trial filter.
- Avoid a mandatory anthropometric/metabolic block because these may be secondary outcomes. Apply no language or date restriction. Use an established animal-only exclusion where appropriate.
- Balance sensitivity and screening burden by testing candidate strings against previously included reports and known 2026 candidates. Verify indexing separately; investigate retrieval failures and document unresolved coverage.
- Check syntax, fields, query interpretation, and export completeness. Record whether independent human peer review was performed; AI-assisted checks are not independent human peer review.
- Preserve final runnable queries and raw exports, dates, counts, batching and any manual record-capture limitations. Record deviations before the affected stage.

### 3.3 Update and original-study reconciliation
Conduct the revised search from inception rather than only after November 2025. Import the original included trial list into the screening database as a verification set. Compare all original inclusions, exclusions, and missed reports against revised PICOS and maintain an old-to-new study map. Screen new reports published after the original search, including the two known 2026 RCT candidates, using the same rules as all other records.

## 4. Deduplication, screening, and linkage

### 4.1 Record handling
Import database exports into Zotero. Apply automatic deduplication followed by manual verification of ambiguous duplicates. Preserve source database, original record ID, DOI/PMID, and duplicate-group provenance. Export a deduplicated screening set with stable `record_id`; retain raw imports and a deduplication summary. Never discard bibliographic provenance solely because a record is a duplicate.

### 4.2 Human–AI workflow
M.S. and M.M. divide records for title/abstract and full-text screening. For each record, one assigned human reviewer independently records include / exclude / unclear, and a reason if excluded. ChatGPT-6 then performs a separate, blinded verification of the same record using the explicit PICOS and a standardized, versioned prompt, without seeing the human decision. All discordant or unclear cases are referred to W.M. for adjudication after review of the underlying source. Both human and AI decisions are retained; the adjudicated decision is final.

The same division plus independent AI verification and W.M. adjudication applies to data extraction and RoB 2. The review manuscript must state accurately that there was one primary human assessment plus AI verification per assigned item.

Because concordance with an LLM is not proof of correctness, implement the following quality checks:

- A human second-check of all full-text exclusions if feasible; log any cases not second-checked.
- A seeded random audit of at least 10% of human–AI-concordant title/abstract exclusions by the other human reviewer (increase the sample if consequential errors are found).
- Human cross-verification of all study-defining characteristics (N, interventions, doses, population, duration) and all main-outcome treatment-effect values before analysis, regardless of AI agreement.


#### Stage 1 exclusion categories
At title/abstract screening, assign each excluded record one primary reason, using the following order when more than one clearly applies:

1. **Ineligible study/report type:** animal or laboratory research, nonrandomized studies, reviews, editorials or other reports without eligible original trial data.
2. **Ineligible population:** exclusively participants under 18 years or pregnant participants. Retain mixed or uncertain populations for full-text clarification under §2.1.
3. **Ineligible intervention/comparator:** no vitamin K supplementation, no eligible randomized comparison, unbalanced co-interventions preventing isolation of the vitamin K effect, or supplementation lasting less than four weeks. Studies of vitamin K antagonists without an eligible supplementation contrast fall here.
4. **No eligible outcome:** clearly no relevant post-intervention anthropometric or body-composition measurement. Absence of an outcome from the abstract alone is insufficient for exclusion.

Retain records with missing or uncertain information for full-text assessment. Do not exclude registry entries, conference abstracts or companion reports solely because of their format. Keep duplicate and retraction counts separate from these eligibility categories. The four category counts must sum to the total excluded at stage 1; retain this breakdown for reporting alongside the PRISMA screening total.


### 4.3 Trial linkage
Use registration numbers, study sites, recruitment dates, sample characteristics, intervention doses, authors, and shared participants to associate reports with a single `study_id`. Create a many-to-one `report_id → study_id` linkage table with evidence and ambiguity notes. Classify reports as primary trial results, secondary analyses, follow-up reports, registry entries, or contextual records. Count each independent randomized population only once in study and participant totals. Document outcomes sourced from multiple reports and handle inconsistent numbers explicitly.

### 4.4 Selection accounting
Reconstruct the entire PRISMA flow from raw records. Verify arithmetic for database and supplementary routes independently: records identified, duplicates removed, screened, excluded, reports sought/retrieved, full-text exclusions by reason, reports included, and distinct RCTs included. Provide a list of apparently eligible but excluded studies and reasons. Do not reuse the old flowchart counts without reconciliation.

## 5. Data extraction and verification
Prepare a standardized, versioned extraction form before re-extraction. Record:

1. **Bibliography and design:** `study_id`, `report_id`, citation, country, registry, allocation/blinding, number of arms, study dates, funding, and conflicts of interest.
2. **Population:** randomized and analyzed N by arm and outcome, sex, age, baseline BMI, clinical condition, exclusions, baseline vitamin K status, relevant medication/co-interventions.
3. **Intervention:** vitamin K form, dose and units, delivery, comparator composition, common supplements, duration, adherence, dropout, and follow-up.
4. **Outcome context:** priority classification, registration/protocol prespecification, sample-size endpoint, measurement method, time point, analysis population, and adjustment variables.
5. **Numerical results:** arm-specific means/SDs or medians/IQRs, change scores and SDs where reported, between-group estimates and 95% CIs/SEs/P values, effect direction, data availability, and source page/table/figure.
6. **Uncertainty:** exact missing items, author contacts, source conflicts, conversions, assumptions, and reviewer/adjudicator notes.

Use a consistent unit system without mislabeling medians as means. Preserve original measures and create derived standardized fields separately. Do not reconstruct participant-level mean body-fat percentage by dividing group mean fat mass by group mean weight. Explicitly distinguish randomized, completed, and outcome-analyzed denominators. For all main results, verify the final analysis dataset directly against the original RCT report and/or companion report.

## 6. Risk-of-bias reassessment
Apply the appropriate Cochrane RoB 2 version to specified results, not one general quality score per RCT. Define the estimand as the effect of assignment to intervention unless a trial design requires another clearly justified target. At minimum, assess each result contributing to a main-outcome synthesis; assess secondary results when needed to interpret substantive claims.

- M.S. and M.M. divide initial assessments; ChatGPT-6 independently verifies signaling-question answers and identifies missing information. W.M. adjudicates disagreements.
- Record every signaling-question answer, source evidence, domain judgment, and overall judgment; save the complete underlying worksheets, not only robvis plots.
- Examine trial registrations, protocols, and analysis plans for prespecification. Lack of a publicly available statistical analysis plan must not automatically be labeled high risk or assumed to prove selective reporting.
- Evaluate missing outcomes/attrition for the specific outcome and distinguish missing data, unclear reporting, inappropriate analysis, and selective reporting.
- Compare newly generated RoB plots against the characteristics table and narrative; counts and named high-risk trials must agree exactly.

## 7. Effect estimates and synthesis plan

### 7.1 Preparation
- Primary continuous effect metric: mean difference (MD) in the natural unit when scales match (kg, kg/m², cm, percentage points). Consider standardized mean differences only for meaningfully comparable constructs measured on incompatible scales, with explicit justification.
- Prefer a reported, correctly adjusted between-group effect and precision. If unavailable, derive an appropriate estimate/SE from arm-level data, change scores, CI, exact P value, or other sufficient statistics with documented formulas. Contact authors when important estimates cannot be reconstructed.
- Do not treat baseline or endpoint means alone, within-arm P values, and between-group treatment effects as interchangeable. Do not assume an unreported change-score SD; if imputation is unavoidable, prespecify plausible correlations and perform sensitivity analyses.
- Preserve study-level direction even for nonsignificant estimates. Do not encode `P ≥ .05` as “no change.”

### 7.2 Feasibility gate for meta-analysis
Assess each main outcome separately after extraction. Pool only if **at least two independent RCTs** provide comparable randomized contrasts, compatible definitions, and effect estimates with usable uncertainty, and pooling answers a clinically interpretable question. Inspect participant populations, vitamin K forms, doses, duration, outcome measures, and risk of bias. Do **not** decide solely on statistical heterogeneity tests or P values. If only two trials are available or heterogeneity is extreme, consider presenting them without a pooled estimate and explain why.

Where justified, use inverse-variance meta-analysis in R with a random-effects model (REML between-study variance) and confidence-interval methods appropriate to the number of studies; assess sensitivity to estimator and small-sample uncertainty. Report study effects and 95% CIs, pooled effects and 95% CIs, τ² and I² when interpretable, and prediction intervals only when supported by enough studies. Avoid unstable subgroup/meta-regression inference from a small number of trials.

Prespecified exploratory questions, subject to adequate data: vitamin K form, intervention duration (≤12 weeks, >12–52 weeks, >52 weeks), baseline vitamin K status, clinical population, and sex composition. Consider sensitivity analyses excluding results at high RoB, secondary/post hoc results, and estimates requiring important imputations. Describe any subgroup comparison as observational across trials; post-treatment “responders” do not constitute a randomized subgroup.

### 7.3 When pooling is inappropriate
Use a SWiM-consistent structured synthesis for each outcome. Present study-specific treatment effects and precision when available, ideally in an unpooled forest plot and a concise study-by-outcome evidence table. Organize studies by meaningful clinical characteristics or risk of bias. If only effect directions can be extracted, use a transparent direction-based synthesis; never vote-count by statistical significance. State what the chosen synthesis can and cannot establish. Do not conclude “no effect” merely because results were nonsignificant.

### 7.4 Missing-results and small-study biases
Search reports and registrations for prespecified outcomes absent from publications; log evidence of unavailable results and author contacts. Evaluate potential selective nonreporting for each outcome. Funnel-plot or asymmetry tests may be considered only for a sufficiently large and suitable meta-analysis (normally at least ten independent studies), with cautious interpretation; otherwise discuss missing-evidence risk qualitatively.

## 8. Certainty of evidence (GRADE)
Assess certainty by main outcome, not by trial. RCT evidence normally begins at high certainty; evaluate risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias. Produce a Summary of Findings table for weight, BMI, waist circumference, fat mass, and body-fat percentage where data allow. If effects cannot be pooled, base judgments on the available study-specific effect estimates and their uncertainty, not on the fraction of nonsignificant studies. Document each downgrade with a short rationale. Avoid implying that a definite absence of effect has been established when the data are compatible with clinically important benefits or harms.
